One professional record across the claim lifecycle

Build the complete claim solution from first inspection through final resolution.

Adjusters and claim professionals can document the loss, produce a traceable estimate, compare carrier scope and settlement figures, explain supported differences, prepare reports or a demand-letter draft, and preserve a handoff-ready case for PAi Legal. A guided homeowner path can begin this same record before the carrier inspection.

The complete professional claim journey

1

Document the loss immediately

Photograph affected areas, narrate a phone walkthrough, add roof or aerial information, import documents, and preserve the original material before repairs or conditions change.

2

Build a preliminary homeowner estimate

Before the carrier adjuster arrives, develop a rough, traceable estimate using visible damage, measurement sources, repair assemblies, public-court-record price anchors, regional factors, and inflation adjustment. It gives the homeowner a prepared point of comparison—not a coverage decision.

3

Import the carrier’s estimate

When the adjuster or carrier provides its estimate, ingest it into the same claim workspace. PAi Claims reads available scope information and keeps the carrier document connected to the comparison.

4

Compare scope and dollars

Identify repair operations that appear included, missing, bundled, or different; compare the carrier replacement-cost figure with the PAi estimate; and track confirmed payments, deductible information, depreciation treatment, and other claim facts.

5

Update the estimate with what changed

Confirm new facts, resolve conflicts, add measurements or supporting documents, and regenerate the estimate so the comparison reflects the current claim record.

6

Build the demand-letter draft

If the claim is still unresolved, PAi Claims uses confirmed damage, the current estimate, policy material, documents, payments, and the calculated amount in dispute to prepare a printable and saveable draft with its supporting enclosures.

7

Confirm before sending

The software identifies facts that still need confirmation, holds back unsupported amounts, and marks the draft out of date if the case or estimate changes. The homeowner decides whether and when to send it. The draft is not legal advice.

8

Continue in PAi Legal if necessary

If payment or resolution still does not occur, export the organized case for PAi Legal. Evidence, estimates, payments, sources, item status, assumptions, revisions, and integrity information move forward instead of forcing the legal workflow to start from disconnected files.

What the comparison can surface

Scope differences

Repair work in the PAi estimate that is not found in the carrier estimate, or carrier lines that require clarification, measurement, or supporting evidence.

Dollar differences

The signed difference between the homeowner’s current estimate and the carrier’s stated replacement-cost amount, with payments and other settlement information shown separately.

Information gaps

Missing policy material, payment records, photos, measurements, identity fields, or conflicting values that should be addressed before producing a demand.

The difference is explained, not merely announced: A higher estimate can result from scope, quantity, regional pricing, inflation, taxes, overhead, repair method, code-related work, or sourced local pricing. PAi Claims keeps those components visible so the request can point to the actual basis.

A demand package built from the case

  • Carrier name, claim number, policyholder, and loss date
  • Current PAi estimate and carrier comparison
  • Confirmed payments received
  • Deductible and depreciation information when available
  • Calculated amount in dispute with qualifications
  • Policy material needed to cite coverage terms and deadlines
  • Photos, estimates, and other supporting enclosures
  • Confirmation list for facts that are not ready to rely on

PAi Claims creates a user-reviewable draft and supporting record. It does not send the letter automatically, provide legal advice, promise payment, or determine litigation strategy.

Why the PAi Legal handoff matters

A dispute is easier to evaluate when the evidence and arithmetic have been organized from the beginning. PAi Claims writes the case files and case graph that PAi Legal expects, stamps the handoff status, records the state revision, and ties the handoff to the saved claim bytes with a SHA-256 identifier.

No re-creating the file

The claim identity, source material, observations, estimates, payments, and reports are already in a structured workspace.

Status travels with the item

An assumption remains identified as an assumption; a confirmed fact remains distinguishable from an unresolved one.

Revision and integrity checks

The handoff can verify that the claim revision and case graph agree and that the referenced claim file matches the recorded hash.

Start before the adjuster—not after a dispute begins.

The earlier the photos, narration, measurements, documents, and estimate are organized, the more useful the comparison and any later demand or legal handoff can become.
