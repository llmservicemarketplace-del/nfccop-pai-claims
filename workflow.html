End-to-end workflow

From raw claim material to a reviewable report.

PAi Claims connects the major stages of claim preparation while keeping the user responsible for reviewing evidence, measurements, scope, pricing, and conclusions.

1

Open a case

Create a workspace for the claim so the property, loss, files, notes, estimate work, and reports stay associated.

2

Capture and import material

Add carrier estimates, contractor material, documents, drone or roof data, and photographs. A phone walkthrough can preserve narration and pictures together for local transcription and photo pairing.

3

Review inputs

Inspect available text, photo observations, scope descriptions, quantities, and missing information. Image interpretation and free-text ambiguity remain review inputs.

4

Develop scope

Map supported damage operations to repair candidates, select catalog items or assemblies, and enter estimate-wide measurements. Separate estimates may be needed for areas with materially different finishes or conditions.

5

Review pricing

Use integrated catalog data, location factors, labor information, and documented local-price overrides. Overrides should identify the source and remain visible for review.

6

Resolve review gates

Address assumptions, uncertain items, unsupported quantities, source gaps, or other flagged issues before relying on the output.

7

Generate outputs

Produce estimate and report material that can explain line items, assumptions, sources, and evidence connections.

Important: PAi Claims can assist with claim preparation and structured estimating, but the user must verify the actual damage, dimensions, repair method, applicable code, local prices, policy terms, and professional conclusions.

Built around review, not blind automation

  • Original evidence remains distinguishable from extracted or interpreted data.
  • Scope candidates are not automatically treated as policy entitlement.
  • Catalog work is kept separate from speculative photo-dollar estimates to reduce double counting.
  • Assumptions and local overrides can remain visible in the work product.
  • Ambiguous inputs can be held for review instead of silently accepted.
  • Reports can communicate limitations and unresolved questions.
