Structured provisional estimating

Build an estimate whose inputs can be reviewed.

PAi Claims combines a repair catalog, assemblies, measurements, public-court-record price anchors, regional market selection, inflation adjustment, labor and location data, and documented local overrides to produce a traceable, location-aware estimate.

Line-item catalog

Select structured repair descriptions, codes, units, and pricing inputs instead of relying only on free-form totals.

Assemblies

Generate related work locally through assembly rules, helping identify connected repair operations while keeping them reviewable.

Measurements

Use estimate-wide dimensions and measures to calculate applicable quantities. Current catalog behavior does not automatically provide area-specific aggregation for rooms with different finishes.

Court-record price anchors

Use repair prices printed in estimates filed in public court records as independent price anchors. Carrier estimates imported into a user's case are compared against the estimate and are not used to set PAi prices.

Regional and inflation adjustment

Resolve the applicable market from available ZIP, state, and regional information, apply labor and material location factors, and bring historical court-record pricing forward with the PAi price index. Sourced local quotes can override a component.

Scope provenance

Retain information about where an estimate item came from and whether it was selected, mapped, generated by an assembly, or entered as an override.

What the software does—and does not do

Capability What PAi Claims supports What still requires verification
Scope mapping Can create provisional scope candidates when a unique catalog item and matching unit are available. Whether the operation is actually required, related to the loss, and covered.
Photo and measurement design AI can identify visible damage and repair context, while dimensions come from measurement reports or user-confirmed measurements. This separation prevents the AI from inventing dimensions from appearance alone and keeps quantities tied to an identifiable measurement source.
Pricing Can apply catalog data, factors, labor inputs, and sourced overrides. Actual local contractor availability, quotes, taxes, code requirements, and market conditions.
Assemblies Can generate related work from configured rules. Whether every generated operation applies to the specific repair.
Totals Can calculate estimate results from selected inputs. Coverage, insurer acceptance, professional reasonableness, and payment entitlement.
Designed for grounded estimating: Price sources, regional selection, inflation adjustment, measurements, assemblies, and overrides remain visible so a user can understand why a line has its quantity and price. Review controls are part of the estimating design, not an afterthought.
