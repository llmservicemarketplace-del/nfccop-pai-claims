Questions and limitations

Know what you are—and are not—buying.

These answers keep the product description honest and distinguish software capabilities from coverage, professional, and claim-payment decisions.

Is PAi Claims an insurance company or public adjuster?

No. It is insurance-claim workspace and estimating software. Software output is not representation by a public adjuster, insurer, attorney, engineer, contractor, or other licensed professional.

Does it determine coverage?

No. Policy language, facts, exclusions, endorsements, applicable law, and carrier decisions require separate review. A line item in an estimate does not establish coverage.

Does it guarantee more claim money?

No. It can help organize evidence and develop reviewable work, but it cannot guarantee a reopened claim, changed scope, increased settlement, acceptance, or payment.

Is the estimate final?

No. Estimates are provisional until measurements, scope, prices, sources, local conditions, code requirements, and assumptions are verified by an appropriate reviewer.

How does PAi Claims combine photos and measurements?

The design assigns different jobs to different evidence: AI helps identify what a photo appears to show, while quantities use measurement reports or user-confirmed dimensions. This prevents an image model from silently inventing measurements and keeps estimate quantities traceable.

Can it read documents?

It can import claim evidence and make extracted text or transcript review material available. Extraction can be incomplete or wrong, so the original document must remain the reference.

How are prices handled?

The PAi price index uses repair prices from estimates filed in public court records, resolves regional or state market information, applies labor and material location factors, and adjusts historical pricing for inflation. Documented local quotes can override applicable components without receiving an additional regional factor.

What are assemblies?

Assemblies are configured rules that can generate related repair operations. Generated work still must be checked against the actual damage and repair method.

What are evidence hashes?

A hash is a digital identifier derived from file content. It can help detect whether the referenced bytes changed, but it does not prove the file is authentic, accurate, lawfully obtained, or admissible.

Should I use a frontier model or private AI?

Frontier models usually provide the strongest document and photo assistance and can work well even on modest computers, but submitted claim content goes to the chosen provider. Private AI runs on the device with no per-use provider charge after a one-time model download. PAi Claims assesses RAM and CPU capacity, shows which local models fit, and recommends an appropriate option.

Is Microsoft Store referral evidence of installation?

No. Clicking a Microsoft Store link is only an outbound referral. Microsoft account or Store reporting would be needed to establish a purchase or installation.

Is free disaster-relief access guaranteed?

No. Any NFCcop offer depends on current availability and published eligibility terms. Free software access also does not guarantee a claim outcome.

What makes the estimate traceable?

The report can show measurements, catalog items, assemblies, public-court-record price anchors, regional selection, inflation adjustment, local overrides, assumptions, and review requirements. That gives the user a visible basis for checking the estimate instead of relying on an unexplained total.

Who should review the output?

That depends on the question. A contractor may review repair methods and costs; a qualified estimator may review quantities and pricing; an engineer may address technical causation or structural issues; an attorney or licensed claim professional may address matters within their authorized scope.
